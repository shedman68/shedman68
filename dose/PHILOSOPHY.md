# The philosophy underneath

The DOSE framework gives this app its content. This document gives it its
*posture* — why it behaves the way it does, and what it refuses to do.

It exists so the design stays honest as features get added. Most of what
follows is not a feature. It's a reason.

---

## 1. You cannot skip the experience

Some processes have no shortcut. The only way to find out what they produce
is to run them, step by step — computational irreducibility. A person is
one of those processes. You cannot think your way to being different; you
have to go through the motions and come out the other side.

**So:** the daily checklist isn't admin. It *is* the computation. The app
never promises transformation from reading, only from doing. Insight is
served in small doses on the way past, never as a substitute for the act.

## 2. Feeling comes before labelling

A child holding an insect is amazed that the thing in their hand is *alive*.
An adult holding the same insect identifies the species, recalls where it
usually lives, and puts it down. The adult knows more and has less.

Labelling is how we look past a thing. Feeling is how we stay with it. And
it is the staying — that inner amazement — that keeps a person searching
for what their life is for. Nobody was ever driven onward by a correct
classification.

Behind the rational account there is something that doesn't reduce: call it
creativity, divinity, the unexplainable. The four chemicals are a label. A
useful one, and still a label.

**So:** the chemistry is scaffolding, never the subject. Where the app can
ask how something *felt* rather than what it *was*, it should. It must never
imply a person is four dials to be optimised — and it should protect the
part of someone that can still be astonished by an ordinary morning.

The test for any new feature: **does this help them notice, or does it help
them categorise?** Prefer noticing.

## 3. Change is a wave, not a line

One becomes many and returns to one. Love turns to hate and back to love.
The Odyssey requires leaving in order to return. Hate has its place in the
cycle; the dip is not a defect in the system, it's part of the shape.

Streak mechanics encode the opposite belief — that value is an unbroken
line and a break is failure. That's a lie about how people actually change.

**So:** the app counts **returns**, not just runs. Coming back after four
days away is the behaviour most worth reinforcing, and it should be
celebrated more warmly than day 30 of an unbroken chain. No shaming of the
gap. The gap was part of it.

## 4. Purpose is where energy points, right now

Humans make purposes; they aren't discovered lying around. And attention,
attachment, anchoring and focus are the same act described four ways —
directing energy at something for a period of time.

That makes purpose seasonal rather than permanent. What you're aimed at
this month is a real answer, not a failed attempt at a final one.

**So:** the pursuit the user names is theirs, in their words, and it is
*expected* to change. The app asks periodically rather than storing a goal
forever and quietly judging them against it.

## 5. Small degrees, and only one at a time

The rhythm that produces consistency: step back, see the whole, hone in on
one thing, return to the daily. Crucially the degree of change per cycle
should be **small**.

**So:** a review surfaces exactly one suggested adjustment. Never a list of
improvements. A list is a way of making someone feel behind.

## 6. Play to strengths

Behaviour change built on shoring up weaknesses is fragile. Built on
amplifying what someone is already drawn to, it compounds.

**So:** the nudge logic is not a deficit model. Mostly it should amplify
what the user is clearly gravitating toward. Occasionally it may raise
something absent — but as a genuine question, not a reprimand:

> You haven't done this in a while. Is that a weakness worth working on,
> or is it simply not for you?

"Not for me" must be a real answer that the app accepts and stops asking
about. Both replies are useful information; only one of them is currently
respected by habit apps.

## 7. Keep the amplitude low

Don't go too deep on the good or the bad. Even tone through both. A
euphoric app on a good day is a punishing app on a bad one — they're the
same dial.

**So:** praise stays warm and brief. Absence is noted without drama. The
app is a steady companion, not a coach and not a cheerleader.

---

## What this rules out

- Streak anxiety, loss-framing, "don't break the chain"
- Scores, grades, percentages, or anything that ranks a day
- Prescribing more than one change at a time
- Treating a skipped action as a failure rather than as information
- Pretending the four chemicals are the whole of a person

## The open question: purpose

The furthest ambition here is to help someone work out what their life is
actually for. That is a much larger venture than a checklist, and it is not
solved by this app in its current form.

Two things make it genuinely hard, and both should be admitted rather than
designed around:

**It is philosophically contested.** Purpose arrives from many angles —
meaning made versus meaning found, duty, craft, service, faith, curiosity —
and they don't reconcile into one flow. Any app that claims a single method
is selling something.

**It has to survive capitalism.** We live in a world where a person's time
is largely already spoken for. A purpose that can't coexist with earning a
living is a purpose most people can't act on. So the honest question isn't
"what would you do if money were no object" — it's how someone directs real
energy toward what matters *inside* the constraints they actually have.

**So, for now:** the app stays on the side of noticing rather than
answering. Its contribution to purpose is the raw material — where your
energy actually goes, what you keep returning to, what you never pick up.
That's the evidence someone needs before any bigger question can be asked
honestly. The pursuit feature should ask better questions over time, and
should not pretend to supply the answer.

## What this asks for, eventually

- Returns celebrated as a first-class event
- A felt check-in alongside the factual one
- A pursuit named by the user, revisited by season
- A step-back review that proposes one small change
- Nudges that amplify strengths and ask honest questions about absences
